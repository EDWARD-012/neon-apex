Police chases in Skidline

In a pursuit there are no laps and no finish line. Patrol cars start a short way behind you, more join the longer it lasts, and the only way to win is to get far enough away and stay there. It is free to play in your browser.

How to escape

Every patrol car has to be more than 300 m away from you along the road, ahead or behind. While they are, the Escape meter at the top left of the screen rises, and six seconds of open road is enough to escape. If a patrol car closes in, the meter drains again. You can also take them out: see takedowns below.

How you get busted

The Busted meter fills while you are almost stopped with a patrol car right next to you, and it is full in two and a half seconds. Get moving and it drains. Stop, and the police creep up and box you in; a warning tells you when that is happening. A car wrecked by crash damage is busted on the spot, wherever it is.

Heat

A chase starts at heat 1 and climbs one level every 40 seconds, up to heat 5. Each level sends its own units after you:

  • Heat 2: a pair of patrol cars. One of them is a blocker, which drives past you and then slows down in your path.
  • Heat 3: a heavy unit, an SUV twice the weight of a patrol car and hard to damage, though slower.
  • Heat 4: another blocker, called in about 260 m ahead of you to wait in your path, and a helicopter. While the helicopter is within 120 m its searchlight keeps you seen, and the Escape meter fills at about a third of its usual rate. It flies at about 210 km/h, so a fast car can leave it behind.
  • Heat 5: two interceptors, sports cars quicker than any car in the game in stock form. An interceptor lined up behind you within 70 m locks on for a second and a half, then fires a pulse that cuts your engine to 15% and stops the nitro for 1.4 seconds. Then it needs 8 seconds to charge again. Swerve out of line while it locks on.

At most seven police cars chase you at once. A quick-race pursuit starts with two patrol cars at heat 1.

Roadblocks and spike strips

From heat 3 the police also set up on the road ahead of you, about 380 m in front, where you cannot see them yet. Every 22 seconds there is something new, roadblocks and spike strips in turn, and a warning comes up as each one goes down.

  • A roadblock is two heavy units parked sideways across the road from one barrier, which leaves a gap of nearly 6 m on the other side. Aim for the gap. Ramming the parked cars works, but they weigh twice what a patrol car does.
  • A spike strip covers one half of the road, with an orange cone at each end. Keep to the other half.
  • From heat 4, the gap in a roadblock can have a spike strip in it too, so the choice is between the spikes and the parked cars.
  • Run over spikes and the tyres are gone for the rest of the pursuit: about 28% off the top speed and 38% off the grip, with sparks from the rims. You can still drive, but it is much harder to stay clear.

These only come in pursuits, not in the chases that start during a race.

Racer tools

Before a pursuit, quick or in the career, pick two tools on the Quick race screen. Each works twice in a pursuit, with a second between uses. Press 1 and 2 (or Q and E, the d-pad's left and right on a gamepad, or the two tool buttons on a phone).

  • Jammer: for 8 seconds the helicopter's searchlight cannot see you, interceptors cannot lock on, and no roadblocks or spike strips go up.
  • Shockwave: every car within 18 m is thrown away from you, harder the closer it is. A police car it sends into a wall counts as your takedown.
  • Turbo: a big push for 2.5 seconds, up to a quarter past your car's top speed.
  • Mine: dropped about 3 m behind you and armed after just over half a second. The first police car to reach it is thrown sideways and badly damaged, often wrecked. It stays on the road for 40 seconds.

Police in races

Races and eliminations can have police too. A patrol car waits at the edge of the road, outside the traffic lanes. Go past within 30 m of it at more than 108 km/h and it gives chase, with the same Busted and Escape meters as a pursuit. If they bust you, your race is over. Get 300 m clear for six seconds and you lose them, which pays 1,000 coins for each heat level, and the patrol cars go back to wait further round the lap. While a chase lasts the heat rises every 40 seconds, and each level's units join as far as the race's limit allows.

In a quick race the Police switch turns this on or off; with it on, up to two patrol cars can chase you. Every career race and elimination has police: up to one patrol car after you in the Street tier, two in Downtown and three in Apex.

How the police drive

A patrol car a long way back follows the racing line a little faster than the planned speed, to close the gap. Once it is close behind, it aims for where you will be half a second later and tries to ram you. If it overshoots, it backs off and waits for you to come past. A blocker looks further ahead, to pull alongside and then in front of you, and once it is ahead it slows in your path to box you in. Patrol cars are quick: in stock form only the Brute V8 has a higher top speed, and interceptors beat even that.

Heavier cars are pushed less in a collision and take less of the knock, so a heavy unit is hard to shove aside. Rams cause damage like any other hit. Armour from the garage cuts the damage from walls and police rams, at the cost of a little weight.

Takedowns

Hit a police car so that, within a second and a half, it slams straight into a wall at more than about 45 km/h, and it is wrecked, with a moment of slow motion. Each one pays 500 coins for every level of heat at the time. In a race, wrecking every police car after you ends the chase at once, as if you had lost them. In a pursuit the wrecked cars drop out, but each new heat level still sends its units while fewer than seven are after you.

What an escape pays

In a quick race, escaping pays 1,000 coins for each level of heat you escaped at, so staying in a chase longer is worth more. Busted pays nothing, not even for near misses or drifts on the way, but it costs nothing either. In the career, each pursuit pays its own reward. Losing the police during a race pays the same 1,000 coins per heat level, every time you do it, and each police car you wreck pays 500 per heat level on top.

Pursuits in the career

  • Blue lights, in the Street tier: two patrol cars, by day, with traffic. 3,500 coins.
  • Heat rising, in the Downtown tier: three patrol cars in the rain, more on the way. 6,000 coins.
  • Full alert, in the Apex tier: four patrol cars from the start, rain and heavy traffic. 10,000 coins.

Tips that follow from the rules

  • Never stop. The Busted meter only fills when you are almost at a standstill.
  • From heat 2, blockers try to get in front of you and slow down. Get past one while you still have speed rather than braking behind it.
  • In a race, a waiting patrol car only reacts if you pass it at more than 108 km/h. Lift off to slip past, or take the chase for the coins.
  • Save nitro for long straights, where it opens the gap fastest.
  • Thread through traffic: each near miss tops the nitro up by 15% of a tank.
  • A glancing touch on a barrier keeps nearly all your speed, so brushing a wall costs less than it looks. Hitting one head-on stops you, and stopping is what gets you busted.
  • Watch the Damage bar. A wreck is an instant bust, so fit armour if you keep getting rammed.
