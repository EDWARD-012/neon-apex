Updates

What has changed in Skidline, newest first. The game updates itself: reload the page and you have the latest version.

21 November 2026

  • Crashes call the police: in a race with police on, a hard ram into traffic or a damaging wall hit sends a patrol car in from behind, and during a chase each crash brings the next heat level sooner. See crashes.
  • A fifth car, the Kestrel S: a light sports coupe for 20,000 coins, class A from the start, with the sharpest turn-in of the five. See the cars.
  • This week's track, the same for everyone from Monday to Sunday, pays 1,000 coins for your first finished run each week, and a daily challenge on the Quick race screen pays 1,500 once a day. See coins.
  • The career is a ladder of crews: win three of a crew's events to race its leader one on one, and pick a reward card the first time you win. Every career event has bronze, silver, gold and Skidline medals that pay once each, and rival cars carry more upgrades in the higher tiers. Press T to restart an event. See the career.
  • Car classes and a rating: every car is class S, A, B, C or D by top speed, with its upgrades counted, and has a rating from 100 to 999. See classes.
  • Looks: a Looks tab in the garage with matte, pearl and chrome finishes and five underglow colours, bought once with coins and then free on every car. See looks.
  • Playing the cop: a new Cop event in Quick race puts you in a police interceptor after four racers. Bust them by ramming or boxing them in, with backup on the radio and two police tools: roadblock, spike strip, helicopter and pulse. Each bust pays 1,000 coins. See playing the cop.
  • Racer tools for pursuits: pick two of jammer, shockwave, turbo and mine on the Quick race screen, and use each twice with 1 and 2. See racer tools.
  • A police helicopter at heat 4 in a pursuit, with a searchlight that slows your escape while it is within 120 m; it flies at about 210 km/h, so you can outrun it. Heat 4 also calls a blocker in ahead of you. At heat 5 the interceptors can lock on from behind and fire a pulse that cuts your power for 1.4 seconds. See heat.
  • Roadblocks and spike strips: from heat 3 in a pursuit, the police set up ahead of you every 22 seconds, a roadblock of two heavy units with a gap on one side, then a spike strip over half the road. From heat 4 the gap can be spiked too. Spikes cost about 28% of top speed and 38% of grip for the rest of the chase. See roadblocks and spike strips.
  • The game is now called Skidline. It was Neon Apex until today; the address stays the same.
  • Heat levels: a chase now climbs to heat 5, one level every 40 seconds. Heat 2 adds a pair of patrol cars, one a blocker that gets in front and slows down; heat 3 a heavy unit; heat 4 another blocker; heat 5 two interceptors. Up to seven police cars chase at once. Heavier cars now push harder in a collision. See police chases.
  • The game sends a few anonymous play events to its own server, such as a race starting or ending, with no account, name or email. The privacy page says what is sent.
  • New sounds, all synthesised: wind that rises with speed, a rush of air when a car passes close, a siren that bends in pitch as a patrol car closes or drops back, a pop on each upshift and a turbo flutter on the Kite RS.
  • Driving aids in Settings: drift assist (the brake-tap drift, on by default), braking assist (off by default) and stability control. See driving aids. Ghost laps from before a handling change are dropped rather than raced against the new handling.
  • A new race screen. The top left shows your place and the cars around you, each with the gap in seconds; the top right shows the race time, lap times and a progress bar. The map that turns with the car moves to the bottom left, with the name of the area under it, and the nitro bar is cut into eight segments. On phones the map stays at the top right, clear of the touch buttons.
  • Drift boost: hold a drift for one second and the sparks turn blue, for three and they turn pink, and straightening up gives a short push a little past top speed. See the drift guide.
  • Kinder walls: a glancing touch on a barrier turns the car to run along it and keeps nearly all its speed. Only a head-on hit stops it.
  • Takedowns: shove a rival or a patrol car into a wall at more than about 45 km/h and it is wrecked. A rival pays 300 coins, a patrol car 500 a heat level. See takedowns.
  • In the cockpit the steering wheel sits lower and closer, so the road is clear above it, and the pillars no longer shine in daylight.
  • Police in races. Races and eliminations can have a patrol car waiting by the road, and it gives chase if you pass it fast. Busted ends the race; losing them pays 1,000 coins a heat level. Every career race and elimination has police, and quick races get a Police switch. See police chases.
  • Wrecks: at full damage your car is wrecked and out of the race, though it keeps the coins earned on the way. Damage also costs up to a fifth of the grip now, and warnings come at 60% and 85%.
  • Every event pays coins now, not only career wins. Quick races pay by place, an escape from the police pays by the heat, and near misses, drift points and clean laps pay on top. A race won by under half a second pays a fifth more, and the first run of the day on today's track pays a bonus that grows with a streak. The results screen lists every line. See coins.
  • The garage has an Upgrades tab: engine, gearbox, nitro, tyres, brakes, suspension, weight and armour, three stages each. See upgrades.

20 November 2026

  • A camera button for phones, next to pause.
  • Brake-tap drift: at speed with the wheel turned hard, a tap of the brake throws the car into a drift. Steering in takes the angle from about 20 to 40 degrees, countersteering brings it down, and straightening up ends it. See the drift guide.
  • Pages about the game, privacy and terms. From this version on the game is all rights reserved.
  • Traffic and patrol cars are now ten everyday car models: sedans, hatchbacks, wagons, vans, SUVs and pickups among them.
  • The four cars you drive are now detailed 3D models by artists on Sketchfab, credited in Settings, and dents show across the whole body.
  • Low graphics draws a plain gradient sky, which fixes a black sky on some computers.
  • Menus and the HUD sit on frosted glass, so they read well over the bright daytime road.
  • Each car has its own engine sound.
  • A cockpit camera, with a dashboard, a steering wheel that turns as you steer and an instrument screen.
  • Handling comes from a tyre model now, with weight moving forward under braking and back under power.
  • A daytime mountain road through a forested valley under snowy peaks. The night city stays as the other choice, and the weather picker offers Day, Night, Rain and Dusk.
  • Accounts: sign in with Google, pick a username and password, and your career is saved online.
  • On a phone held upright the game asks you to turn it sideways, and on Android a race goes full screen.
  • The game moved to apex.edward7780.com. The old address forwards here, shared track links included.
  • Crash damage: hits dent the panel they land on, scuff the paint, knock off mirrors and wings and can put a headlight out, and a badly damaged car smokes and then burns. Damage costs up to 30% of engine power and 12% of top speed.
  • More cameras: far chase, bonnet, bumper and drone, and V to look back. A bigger nitro.

18 November 2026: the first version

  • Tracks generated from a seed, AI rivals on a computed racing line, car physics and race rules. Laps count only when every checkpoint is passed in order.
  • A 3D city at night, with clear, rain and dusk weather, light trails and a speed blur.
  • Four cars, traffic with near misses, and police. Five kinds of event: race, elimination, drift, pursuit and time trial.
  • A career of twelve events in three tiers, with cash and unlocks.
  • A fly-in before each race, slow motion at the finish, and replays.
  • Menus for quick race, career, garage and replays, synthesised sound, and keyboard, gamepad and touch controls.
  • A half-minute trailer.
