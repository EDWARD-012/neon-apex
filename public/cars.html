Cars and upgrades in Skidline

There are nine cars, each with its own handling and engine sound. You start with the Vanta GT and buy the other eight with coins from races. Every car takes eight upgrade parts with three stages each, and a stage changes the numbers the physics runs on, so an upgraded car really is quicker on the road.

The nine cars

Vanta GT: free, your first car

"Balanced. Nothing to fear, nothing it is best at." Every other car in the game is the Vanta with a few numbers changed, so it is the one to learn on. It sounds like a high, smooth twelve-cylinder.

Kite RS: 6,000 coins

"Light hatch with sticky tyres. Slow on the straights, untouchable in the bends." It has the most grip of all nine and, with the Kestrel, the Finch and the Marten, the most steering lock, and it rates lowest for drifting. At 238 km/h it is quicker than only the Finch, the Moraine and the Granite. It sounds like a buzzy turbo four with a whistle that rises with boost.

Brute V8: 14,000 coins

"Muscle car. Huge top speed and a rear end that wants to come round." The highest top speed of all nine and the hardest nitro push, but the least grip of the road cars and a little less pull than the Vanta. It rates highest for drifting. It sounds like a low, lumpy eight with a loping idle.

Volt E: 26,000 coins

"Electric. Instant pull off every corner and a nitro tank that lasts." The strongest pull of all nine and the slowest-burning nitro tank, though each burst pushes a little less hard than the Vanta's. It sounds like an electric motor's whine.

Kestrel S: 20,000 coins

"Light sports coupe. Turns in sharper than any of the others and pulls hard, but it skates when pushed." It turns in quickest of all nine, has as much steering lock as the Kite, more pull than the Vanta and the second-highest top speed, 270 km/h, which puts it in class A from the start. It can corner a little harder than the Vanta, but its tyres let go sooner once it starts to slide, so it skates more readily. It is the sports car from the traffic pack, painted in your colour, and it sounds like a turbo four.

Granite T: 6,500 coins

"Pickup. Slow and heavy, but nothing pushes it around and walls barely mark it." The slowest car in the garage at 209 km/h, class D, and the heaviest and toughest: rivals and patrol cars bounce off it, and the same wall hit does it the least damage. It is the traffic pack's pickup in your paint and sounds like a V8.

Moraine X: 7,500 coins

"SUV. Sits high and stays calm, quicker than the pickup and nearly as hard to push around." 216 km/h, class D, second only to the Granite for weight and toughness. It is the traffic pack's off-roader in your paint and sounds like a V8.

Finch HX: 8,000 coins

"Hot hatch. Light, grippy and eager to change direction, but it runs out of road at the top end." Only the Kite has more grip, but at 230 km/h it is the slowest of the road cars, and being light it gets pushed around and takes the most damage from a hit. It is the traffic pack's hatchback in your paint and sounds like a turbo four.

Marten R: 11,000 coins

"Rally car. Pulls hard out of slow corners and holds a slide at any angle, and it is built to take a knock." Only the Volt pulls harder, it rates as well as the Kestrel for drifting, and it takes less damage from a hit than any of the road cars. 241 km/h, class C. It is the traffic pack's coupe in your paint and sounds like a turbo four.

Upgrades

Open the Garage and switch to the Upgrades tab. Each part has three stages, bought in order and only for a car you own. A stage costs a share of the car's price, so cheap cars are cheap to tune. The Vanta GT is free, so it is priced as if it cost 10,000. On the Kite RS, for example, engine stage 1 costs 900 coins and stage 3 costs 3,000.

Tuning

Under the parts on the Upgrades tab are four sliders, free to move on a car you own and kept with that car. Each stays within a small range, so a setup changes how the car feels without turning it into a different car. Let go of a slider to keep it; Reset tuning puts them all back in the middle.

  • Gearing, once the car has a gearbox stage: shorter gears pull up to 8% harder, longer ones add up to 4% to the top speed, and you trade one for the other.
  • Brake balance, once it has a brakes stage: toward the rear the car swings into a drift more easily and stops a little later; toward the front it stays planted.
  • Camber, once it has a suspension stage: down to −3°, the car corners up to 5% harder but brakes up to 5% worse in a straight line.
  • Steering lock, from the start: more lock turns up to 10% tighter and quicker, but the car gets twitchier.

Classes and rating

Every car has a class from its top speed, with its upgrades counted: S from 295 km/h, A from 270, B from 245, C from 220 and D below that. Stock, the Brute V8 is class A, the Vanta GT and the Volt E are B, and the Kite RS is C. Upgrades can lift a car a class: a stage 3 engine takes the Vanta GT to 290 km/h and class A, the Kite RS to 266 km/h and class B, and the Brute V8 to 314 km/h and class S.

Next to the class the garage shows a rating from 100 to 999. It weighs top speed most, then pull and grip, then braking, and every upgrade that touches those raises it.

Looks

The garage's Looks tab has paint finishes (gloss, matte, pearl and chrome), underglow colours, and liveries: stripes, a side flash, a chequered band or race numbers, in white, black, red, yellow or cyan, plus wheels, ride height, window tint and a spoiler. Your car's number plates show your username. Try one on the turntable before you buy it. The garage's Photo button hides the menu so you can turn the camera round your car, zoom, and save a picture of it. A look is bought once and then works on every car you own. Prices are on the coins page.
