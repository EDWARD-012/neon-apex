Skidline privacy

You can play Skidline without an account. Your settings, career progress, cars and ghost laps are then kept only in your browser's local storage, on your device.

If you sign in with Google, the game receives your Google account's ID number, email address and whether that address is verified. It stores the ID number and the email address so it can recognise you next time. It does not see your Google password and asks for nothing else.

On your first sign-in you choose a username and a password for signing in on other devices. The password is stored only as a salted scrypt hash, never as written.

For a signed-in player the server also stores the career save: coins, cars owned, upgrades fitted, events won and the daily-track streak. Nothing is sold, shared or used for advertising.

When you finish a time trial signed in, the game sends what you pressed on every step of the physics, with your car, its upgrades and tuning, and the damage it started with. The server drives the lap again to check it, then keeps your best lap on each track: its time, the car and its upgrades. Your username, lap time and car are shown on that track's public leaderboard. For laps that other players are likely to race as a ghost, the server also keeps the run itself, so the game can rebuild the lap. Those are laps in a board's top 10, laps whose driver has been added as a friend by someone with a lap on the same track, and laps in a challenge. Runs on a daily track are dropped after 7 days, and on a weekly track after 5 weeks.

If you use Friends, the server stores the usernames you add, and when you added them. You add someone without asking them. They can see that you added them, so they can add you back; otherwise your list of friends is shown only to you. Only two players who have added each other can send each other challenges. A challenge stores who sent it, to whom, the track, the sender's lap time and when it was sent. It is deleted when the person who got it dismisses it, or after 30 days.

To see how the game is played, it also sends a few anonymous play events to its own server: when the game is opened (whether the screen is a touch screen, the graphics setting and the screen width, rounded), when a race starts and ends (the kind of event, the car, the weather, how long it lasted, your place, the coins it paid, and whether it ended in a wreck or an arrest), and a ping for each minute of play. They carry a random ID made in your browser and kept in its local storage. That ID is not linked to your account, name or email, and the game's database does not store your IP address with the events. There are no third-party analytics or advertising scripts. Clearing this site's data in your browser gives you a new random ID.

A cookie named na_session keeps you signed in for up to 90 days. Signing out removes it.

The game runs on Vercel and the database on Neon. To have your account deleted, email ravi5258p@gmail.com from the address you signed in with, and it will be removed, along with your laps, friends and challenges.
