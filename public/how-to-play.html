How to play Skidline

Skidline runs in the browser on a computer or a phone. Pick Quick race or Career from the menu, let the camera fly in over the track (any key skips it), and go when the light turns green.

Keyboard steering comes in over a fraction of a second rather than all at once, so a short tap on an arrow key makes a small correction and holding it gives full lock.

Gamepad

Plug in a controller and it works next to the keyboard: whatever you press on it takes over. The triggers are analogue, so half a press is half throttle. Buttons are named for an Xbox pad, with the PlayStation button in brackets.

Phone and tablet

Hold the phone sideways. The game is played in landscape, and on Android it goes full screen when a race starts.

  • Steer by dragging the half wheel at the bottom left with your thumb: the further you drag, the more the wheels turn, and it springs back to the middle when you let go. Settings has two other ways, two arrow buttons or tilting the phone.
  • The accelerator is the tall pedal at the bottom right. Pressing near its bottom is part throttle and near its top full throttle, and it glows as the revs climb. The wider pedal next to it is the brake, and you can slide your thumb from one to the other without lifting it.
  • Nitro and Drift sit to the left of the pedals. A ring round Nitro shows how full the tank is, and Drift lights up blue, then pink, as a drift charges its boost.
  • Pause and Camera are at the top. The pause menu also has Look back, Back on the road and Restart, the things a keyboard has keys for.
  • On Android the phone gives a short buzz for a hard hit, a takedown and nitro firing. iPhones don't allow it.
  • Settings has a left-handed layout that puts the wheel on the right and the pedals on the left.

If the game feels slow, set Graphics to Low in Settings. The Settings screen also has the full controls chart for keyboard, gamepad and phone.

The perfect start

Put the throttle down in the last moment before the light turns green, within the final 0.45 seconds of the countdown. Holding it down from earlier does not count. Get it right and "Perfect start" flashes up and 30% of a tank goes into your nitro.

Nitro

Hold Shift or N with the throttle down to burn nitro. On the Vanta GT it multiplies the engine's pull by 1.75 and lifts the top speed by 22% while it burns. The Brute V8 gets the hardest push of the four cars, and the Volt E's tank lasts the longest.

The tank starts every race 40% full. Drifting refills it fastest, and the wider the angle the quicker it fills. Each near miss adds 15% of a tank, a perfect start adds 30%, and tucking in close behind a rival at speed, in its slipstream, adds 6% a second. When you are not using it the tank slowly creeps back up on its own.

Near misses

Pass a traffic car close alongside, clearly faster than it, without touching it, and you score a near miss: 50 coins, 15% of a tank of nitro and a beat of slow motion. If you brushed the car just before passing it, it does not count. You can turn traffic off for a quick race, and some career events have plenty of it.

Drifting

At speed, turn hard and tap the brake. The tail swings out and the car drifts. Keep the throttle down and steer into the corner to hold it, and straighten the wheel to end it. Space does the same job as the brake tap. Hold a drift for a second or more and straightening up gives a short boost. The drift guide covers points, the boost, angles and which car slides best.

Cameras, including the cockpit view

Press C to go through the six driving cameras: chase, far chase, cockpit, bonnet, bumper and drone. The cockpit view sits at the driver's eye, on the left or the right depending on the car. The wheel turns as you steer, the rev counter and speedo have lit needles with the gear between them and shift lights above, the centre screen shows the map, and your head moves with the car in corners and under braking. On high quality the mirrors show the road behind. Hold V to look behind you. After a race, Watch replay plays it back from TV, chase, heli and bumper cameras.

Crashes and damage

Hits on walls and other cars dent the body where they land, scuff the paint, and can knock off mirrors and wings or put a headlight out. A badly damaged car smokes and then burns. The Damage bar in the bottom right corner shows how bad it is. Damage costs up to 30% of the engine's power, 12% of the top speed and a fifth of the grip, and warnings pop up at 60% and 85%. At full damage the car is wrecked: in a race it is out, though it keeps the coins it earned on the way, and in a police pursuit it is busted. Armour from the garage means less damage from every hit.

Walls are forgiving at a shallow angle: a glancing touch turns the car to run along the barrier and keeps nearly all its speed. Only a hit straight into the wall stops it.

Stuck against a wall or facing the wrong way? R puts the car back in the middle of the road, stopped and pointing the right way. It does not fix the damage.

Takedowns

Hit a rival or a police car so that, within a second and a half, it slams straight into a wall at more than about 45 km/h, and it is wrecked, with a moment of slow motion. A rival pays 300 coins and a police car 500 for each heat level. Traffic can't be taken down; it is only in the way.

Driving aids

Settings has three driving aids:

  • Drift assist, on by default, is the brake-tap drift described above. With it off, a brake tap is just braking, and Space works as a handbrake that locks the rear wheels; throttle with the wheel turned holds the slide.
  • Braking assist, off by default, brakes for you when the car is more than 8% faster than the racing line's speed about a second ahead.
  • Stability, on by default. Turn it off to drive on the raw tyre model.

Graphics: Auto, High and Low

Settings has three graphics choices. Auto, the default, times the first seconds of each race. If the game runs slower than 40 frames a second it drops from High to Low, and if Low still runs below 28 frames a second it draws the picture at 85% and then 70% of its size. Auto only ever steps down, remembers what it picked for your device, and Settings says what that was. Pick High or Low to set it yourself. Low, which phones start on, also loads half-size photos for the road, rocks and ground, about 360 KB instead of 5.2 MB, so it is quicker to download.

Saving your progress

Coins, cars, upgrades and career progress are saved in your browser as you play. To keep them on every device, open Account from the menu and sign in with Google, then pick a username and password for signing in elsewhere.
