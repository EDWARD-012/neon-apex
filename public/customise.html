Car customisation and tuning in Skidline

Every car in Skidline can be painted, dressed up and tuned in the garage. Paint colours and the wrap painter are free. Finishes, underglow, liveries, wheels, ride height, tint and spoilers cost coins, and each is bought once and then works on every car you own. The tuning sliders are free too, and change how the car drives.

Open the garage

Looks

Open the Garage and switch to the Looks tab. You can try anything on the car on the turntable before you buy it, and a look you have bought is free on every other car you own. None of the looks changes the physics: a spoiler or lowered ride is for the eye only.

A livery comes in white, black, red, yellow or cyan, and you can change its colour and the race number at any time for free. Your car's number plates carry your username when you are signed in. New wheels replace the whole wheel, tyre included, at the size of the one they replace. Tint darkens only the windows, so the headlights stay clear.

The wrap painter

At the bottom of the Looks tab is the wrap painter, which costs nothing. A wrap is built from up to six layers, drawn in order on top of the livery. For each layer you pick:

  • a shape: a stripe, a circle, a star, a chevron, or your own text of up to 12 characters;
  • where it goes: both sides, the left or right side only, the bonnet or the roof;
  • one of nine colours;
  • its size, where it sits along the car and how high (or across the top, on the bonnet and roof), and a turn of up to 45 degrees either way.

The sliders show the change on the car as you move them, and letting go keeps it. The wrap is saved with that car, and goes with your save to every device if you are signed in.

Photo mode

The garage's Photo button hides the menu and stops the turntable. Drag to turn the camera round your car, and zoom with the mouse wheel or a pinch. Save picture downloads a PNG of the view with your car's name and the game's in a corner. Escape or Done brings the garage back.

Tuning sliders

The Upgrades tab has four sliders under the eight upgrade parts. They are free to move on any car you own, they are kept with that car, and each stays within a small range, so a setup changes how a car feels without turning it into another car. Moving a slider shows its effect on the car's bars; letting go keeps it, and Reset tuning puts them all back in the middle.

SliderOpens withWhat it does
GearingA gearbox stageShorter gears pull up to 8% harder; longer ones add up to 4% to the top speed. You trade one for the other.
Brake balanceA brakes stageToward the rear the car swings into a drift more easily and stops a little later; toward the front it stays planted.
CamberA suspension stageDown to −3°, the car corners up to 5% harder but brakes up to 5% worse in a straight line.
Steering lockFrom the startMore lock turns up to 10% tighter and quicker, but makes the car twitchier.

Tuning counts on the leaderboards: a time-trial lap is checked with the car's upgrades and tuning as you drove it. More on the leaderboards page.

More in the garage

The upgrade parts themselves (engine, gearbox, nitro, tyres, brakes, suspension, weight and armour, three stages each), every car's top speed with each engine stage, and the S to D classes are on the cars page. Coins for all of it come from every race; the coins page lists what pays.
